# Medical Coding and Narrative Quality
## Pharmacovigilance - Task 02

### 1. Objective
To standardize reported product and adverse event terms,
map events to appropriate medical coding terms, and prepare
chronological case narratives using only supplied facts.

### 2. Case 1: PV-101

Patient: 34-year-old female
Country: India
Suspected Product: Velunex
Reported Event: Nausea
Onset After Use: 3 hours
Outcome: Recovered
Seriousness: Reported as not serious
Concomitant Information: None reported

Medical Coding:
Reported term: Nausea
Proposed MedDRA Preferred Term: Nausea
Product active ingredient: Not provided
Dose and route: Not reported
Indication: Not reported

Narrative:
A 34-year-old female from India reportedly experienced nausea
three hours after using Velunex. The reported outcome was
recovery. The supplied dataset classifies the event as not
serious. Dose, route, indication, exact dates, and reporter
details were not provided.

### 6. Data Quality and Missing Information

- Product active ingredient and product identity require verification.
- Dose, route, and indication were not supplied.
- Exact medication start dates and event dates were unavailable.
- Reporter details were not supplied.
- Additional clinical details and relevant medical history
  require follow-up.
- Causality cannot be established from timing alone.
- Seriousness classifications are recorded as supplied and
  require review against applicable criteria.

### 7. Coding Limitations

The proposed medical terms are preliminary mappings based on
the reported event descriptions. Final MedDRA coding requires
verification using the applicable MedDRA version and available
coding tools. Product coding cannot be completed without
verified product information.

### 8. Conclusion

The reported events have been organized into structured case
summaries and chronological narratives. Missing information
has been documented without inventing facts. Additional
information is required to finalize product coding and assess
the cases fully.
