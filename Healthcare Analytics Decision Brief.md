# Healthcare Analytics Decision Brief

## 1. Executive Summary

This report combines synthetic patient records, pharmacy sales data,
and adverse-event reporting data to identify operational priorities.

The analysis focuses on patient readmission, pharmacy revenue,
product mix, and medication safety reporting.

All records are fictional and are used for educational purposes only.

## 2. Operational Question

How can healthcare teams improve patient follow-up, pharmacy
operations, and medication-safety monitoring using available data?

## 3. Patient Dataset Analysis

Total patient records reviewed: 10

Patients with readmission recorded as "Yes":
- PAT-205
- PAT-209

Total readmissions recorded: 2

Readmission proportion:
2 / 10 × 100 = 20%

This is a descriptive result for the supplied sample only.
It does not establish the cause of readmission or predict future risk.

### Recommended Actions
- Review the records of patients with readmission.
- Check whether discharge follow-up was documented.
- Review relevant clinical factors with qualified healthcare staff.
- Avoid drawing conclusions from the small sample.

## 4. Pharmacy Sales Analysis

Total transactions: 10

Total recorded sales revenue: INR 54,260

### Branded Products

Recorded revenue:
- Amoxicillin 500 mg: INR 3,825
- Atorvastatin 10 mg: INR 8,800
- Azithromycin 500 mg: INR 7,800
- Vitamin D3 60K: INR 15,750

Total branded revenue: INR 36,175

### Generic Products

Total generic revenue: INR 18,085

### Product Mix

Branded revenue proportion:
36,175 / 54,260 × 100 = 66.66%

Generic revenue proportion:
18,085 / 54,260 × 100 = 33.34%

These figures describe recorded revenue, not profit.
Product cost data are unavailable, so profit margins cannot be calculated.

### Recommended Actions
- Monitor stock availability for frequently sold products.
- Review branded and generic product sales separately.
- Check procurement costs before making margin-related decisions.
- Avoid recommending a medicine solely on the basis of sales.

## 5. Medication Safety and Adverse-Event Reporting

The supplied adverse-event dataset contains eight reports.

Reported serious events should receive appropriate review according
to applicable pharmacovigilance procedures.

High report counts alone do not prove that a drug caused an event.

### Recommended Actions
- Validate drug, reaction, severity, outcome, and patient fields.
- Review serious reports using the appropriate safety workflow.
- Check missing or inconsistent information.
- Refer potential safety signals to qualified pharmacovigilance reviewers.

## 6. Combined Findings

The available datasets support three operational priorities:

1. Review patient records with documented readmission.
2. Monitor pharmacy sales and stock availability using validated data.
3. Review adverse-event reports for completeness and potential signals.

These findings are descriptive and should not be interpreted as proof
of treatment effectiveness, medication causality, or clinical risk.

## 7. Privacy, Bias, and Data Quality

- The datasets are synthetic and intended for educational analysis.
- Patient information should be handled according to privacy rules.
- The patient sample is small and may not represent a wider population.
- Missing clinical details may affect interpretation.
- Pharmacy revenue does not provide information about profit or treatment quality.
- Adverse-event reports may be incomplete and do not establish causation.
- Findings should be validated before operational decisions are made.

## 8. Conclusion

The combined analysis provides a starting point for reviewing
readmission records, pharmacy sales, and medication-safety reports.

Further validation and expert review are required before making
clinical, financial, or regulatory decisions.

## 9. Limitations

This is a small, synthetic dataset. The results cannot be generalized
to all patients, pharmacies, or medicines.

No unsupported medical or causal claims are made.
